A long drive, a server-checked finish and a public prize pool.

01

Thesis

Attention is the scarce good. A public clock, a public pool and a public board sell it.

Desert Bus (1995) was an eight-hour drive from Tucson to Las Vegas at 45 mph for one point. People finished it, and others watched, because the cost was real time in public. Freight Chain keeps that drive and adds a server-verified finish and a program-held skill pool:

  1. Time is the price. A shift completes at its server-set finish line. The truck is governed at 28 m/s, so distance takes time; idling and a hidden tab earn no progress.
  2. The finish is not self-reported. The browser reports the drive; the server checks 10 Hz samples, frame-level traffic proof and live events against shared road and simulation rules (06).
  3. Money is held by rules, not by us. Tickets and base funding sit in a program-owned vault (85% weekly boards · 5% rollover · 10% Season Final, with player-derived funding < 50%) and a permissionless refund path (05).
  4. Everything is watchable. Any ride can be streamed live or replayed from the same recording the verdict judges. Spectators can chat and tip the driver (07).

WHO IT IS FOR

Players who enjoy manual-gearbox sims and endurance challenges, stream viewers, and Solana users who want verifiable skill pools.

WHAT IT IS NOT

Not a house game or yield product: results depend strictly on driving skill with equal tie-splitting, and operator + sponsor base funding always exceeds the player-derived bonus (< 50% player share).

WHERE IT RUNS

In a desktop browser, no download. Wallet: Phantom, Solflare, Backpack, or QR phone-to-desktop pairing. Network: Solana devnet today (Pre-season Nov 2026; Season 1 mainnet Jan 2027).

02

How to drive

Inputs, indicators and advice. The truck is a long-hood semi with a seven-speed manual gearbox, a cold engine and a handbrake that is on when you start.

THE FIRST MINUTE · STEP BY STEP

Nine controls take you from a dead dash to moving. Each card is the real cockpit, drawn by the game's own code: hover it (tap on a phone) to work the control. On a free ride, TUTORIAL teaches it, ASSIST (not on HARD) doubles the truck's endurance, and finishing without it earns On My Own.

  1. KEY ON

    Turn the ignition to ON: click the ON zone of the key. A one-second self-test sweeps every gauge and lamp. With the key OFF the whole dash, even the brake pedal, is dead.

  2. START

    Press I for a one-tap start, or hold the START zone of the key for about a second. The engine cranks, catches with a rev flare and settles at idle. O switches it off; I again after a stall.

  3. PARK BRAKE OFF

    Press B or click the red PARK octagon. It is ON at the start of every ride and holds even against the engine. Driving against it cooks the brakes.

  4. CLUTCH IN

    Hold Shift or Space; the CLU bar shows how far it is in. It must be in to change gear. Ease it out while you add gas: let it go too fast at low revs and the engine stalls.

  5. SELECT A GEAR

    1–7 select a gear, 0 neutral; E up, Q down. Clutch in first, or the box grinds. Pull away in 1st or 2nd.

  6. GAS

    Hold W or ↑; the GAS bar follows the pedal. Torque peaks around 2000 rpm and is nearly gone at 1200, so a tall gear at a crawl lugs and stalls.

  7. STEER

    A D or ← → turn the wheel. Steering is stronger at walking pace, so the depot turn is possible.

  8. BRAKE

    Hold S or ↓; the BRK bar shows the pedal. It does nothing with the engine off or stalled, the air system needs it running. Hard stops from speed heat the brakes.

  9. SHIFT UP

    Watch the tach: clutch in and E at about 2300 rpm. Keep the revs between 1000 and 2500; sitting on the redline charges STRAIN.

WHEN YOU NEED THEM

The nine steps are an order. These six are conditions: it gets dark, the glass frosts or fogs, you want company on the road, or you have to back up.

HEADLIGHTS & NIGHT

L headlights (or click HEAD). Night comes round on its own; then the headlights are the only light on the road, and they drain the battery.

CAB LIGHT

C, or click the dome lamp in the overhead console. It lights the cab, works only with the engine running or starting, and goes out when the engine stops.

HEAT & COLD

H or HEAT melts frost from the vents; the bar is how warm the air is. J or COLD clears fog above 18 °C and draws the same battery current. The compressor needs the engine running. Both can be on.

WIPERS & WASHER

R cycles off / slow / fast, hold X to spray. Dry blades barely clear dust, so wash first. Nothing but the heater clears frost, and nothing but cooling clears fog.

RADIO

T on / off, , . station, − = volume. Needs the key ON. Eleven instrumental stations; everyone hears the same song at the same moment.

REVERSE

Q from neutral, or G. Stop first: forward ↔ reverse while still rolling grinds. The trailer swings out and the truck beeps.

Also: P pauses (then END SHIFT), M mutes, K folds the spectator chat panel (07), N the phone map. Every dash button (HEAD, CAB, HEAT, COLD, WIPER, WASH, PARK, the key, the radio, the phone) can also be clicked.

INDICATORS · READ THE DASH

Nine things to watch while you drive. They move the same way: hover or tap.

SPEEDOMETER

Speed, 0–120 km/h. The governor stops the truck at about 100 km/h; nothing gets you past it, so do not chase it.

TACHOMETER

RPM×100. Idle 700, red zone from 2800. Below about 450 in gear with the clutch out, it stalls.

GEAR WINDOW

Current gear, amber while the engine runs: N, 1–7, R. Seven gears, ratio 6.15 → 0.75.

ENGINE GAUGES

TEMP in °C, red from about 98 °C, plateaus at 85–95. OIL and AIR follow the engine; H2O is the washer tank, it drains while you spray and refills slowly.

VOLT & FUEL

VOLT is the battery: lights, heater, cooling, radio and instruments drain it, the alternator refills it only while the engine runs, and with the engine off it drains much faster. FUEL drifts down with distance as a display – the truck never runs dry.

TRIP LCD

Kilometres driven, shift time over the minimum and outside °C. The server's clock is the one that counts; this one is your guide.

PEDALS & HULL

CLU, BRK and GAS are the pedals, HUL is hull integrity in %. Below 100 % power is cut (down to 40 %) and the steering pulls to one side; 0 % ends the shift.

LAMPS · ENGINE

STALL, COLD, STRAIN, BRAKES. Amber means watch it, red means fix it now, blinking red means seconds left.

WARNING LAMPS

Amber means watch it, red means fix it now, blinking red means seconds left.

STALL

Gear in, clutch mostly out and revs under 450. Clutch in, restart with I.

COLD

The engine is below working temperature: less power, and revving high costs hull. Drive gently until it goes out.

STRAIN

Charges while you hold 92 % of redline or more; full means the engine blows and the shift ends. Shift up. (Charge time is a test value today.)

OFF ROAD

Wheels off the tarmac, sand drags the truck. Time accumulates and drains twice as fast once you are back; about 20 s in total loses the ride.

BRAKES

Hot brakes: hard stops from speed, or driving against a held brake. Hot brakes fade, the parking brake too. Full strain ends the shift.

OVER-REV

Revs above the red zone. Change up or lift.

GRIND

A shift attempted without the clutch, or forward ↔ reverse while still rolling.

BAT

Battery below 30 % (red and blinking below 15 %). The engine will not crank below 12 %. Flat, the lights, heater, cooling and radio cut out, then the battery recovers. The ride goes on.

ADVICE FROM THE LONG HAUL

  1. Release the parking brake first. Driving against it cooks the brakes and charges STRAIN; with the pedal instead, the engine just stalls.
  2. Start gently in the cold. On the Trans-Siberian route (−22 °C) idle alone never warms the engine; driving gently does. Do not rev a COLD engine.
  3. Pick the gear for the speed. 30 km/h wants 4th. A tall gear at a crawl lugs and stalls instead of pulling; standing starts belong to 1st or 2nd.
  4. Do not live on the redline. Upshift near 2300 rpm. STRAIN charges above 92 % of redline.
  5. Straddle potholes, slow for trenches. A yellow sign comes about 90 m before every hole. A small pothole can be passed between the wheels; a trench or sinkhole needs a lane change or a crawl.
  6. Keep the truck on the road. Poles, guardrails, cacti and other trucks are solid; scratches and bumps cost hull, a direct hit ends the shift.
  7. Lights at night. With no lights the world is black outside the lamp posts; oncoming traffic dazzles you. At night the headlights are the only thing lighting the road.
  8. Mind the battery. With the engine off, lights, heater, cooling and radio flatten it in about a minute. Switch everything off and it recovers a little; headlights plus the heater or the cooler out-drain the alternator even while driving.
  9. Wash the glass, then wipe. Dust builds up; the washer makes each wiper pass clear far more. Frost needs the heater. Fog, above 18 °C, needs cooling. Wipers clear neither.
  10. Do not alt-tab on a paid shift. A hidden tab pauses the game and earns nothing; the server will not credit those seconds.
  11. Respect the crossroads. Junctions have signals and cross traffic, and a direct hit with another vehicle ends the shift on the spot.

FIVE ROUTES

All routes share the same rules and road generator; they change scenery, weather and the bend style. The server picks each ride's route and start hour from its seed. The road is generated endlessly from a seed (crossroads, one to three lanes per direction, towns, lights).

  • Route 66 · USA sand, saguaros, gentle bends
  • Stuart Highway · Australia red earth, nearly straight, red dust
  • Trans-Siberian · Russia snow, −22 °C, frost and short visibility
  • Brenner Pass · Alps tight serpentines, guardrails
  • Pan-American · Atacama salt flats, long sweepers, thin bright air

Above 18 °C the windscreen fogs until cooling is on: Route 66 by day, and Stuart Highway day or night. Siberia frosts until the heater blows warm. Brenner frosts at night and does not fog. Pan-American does neither.

  1. 1SIGN IN1-click Sign-In With Solana (solana:signIn, CAIP-122), legacy signMessage fallback, or QR phone-to-desktop pairing (/api/auth/pair). .sol names resolve on chain via SNS.

  4. 4FINISHReach the server-set distance (½ × 28 m/s × nominal length on paid shifts; no minimum time gate) and the shift is complete. Free levels end automatically; paid drivers end via P → END SHIFT.

HOW IT ENDS

COMPLETED

Finish line reached, then the ride ended. The recording is judged (06). Paid boards require a verified verdict when enforcement is enabled; the production default is shadow mode.

ABANDONED

You ended it before it completed. No rank. A paid ticket is spent.

FAILED

A direct hit, hull at 0 %, a blown engine or drivetrain, about 20 s off the road, no heartbeat for 150 s (the tab is gone), or a reported distance faster than the truck can go.

RULES OF PARTICIPATION

  1. No wallet needed to watch or try EASY. Watching streams, reading boards, and the guest EASY ride need no sign-in. Ranked shifts, chat, and tips need one signature.
  2. One person, one wallet. Tickets, badges, TEN4 and rank belong to the wallet that signed in. Multi-accounting or account sharing disqualifies all linked entries.
  3. Ticket spend & no-refund rule. We and sponsors fund the major pool (> 50%), and optional paid tickets (< 50% cap) buy extra ranked attempts. All ticket purchases are final and non-refundable once bought—there is no minimum ticket-sale goal refund. A ticket is spent when the shift starts, and unused tickets expire and burn at the pool's start cut-off (last_start_at), refunded only on operator cancellation, a server outage, or 14-day unsettled expiry (05). Starting while a ride is open resumes it.
  4. Weekly ranked cap. Up to 5 paid + 1 promo ranked starts per wallet per week across the Mon 00:00 – Sun 12:00 UTC window.
  5. Drive by hand in the real client. Scripted timing flags for human review; impossible physics or missing/altered traffic proof rejects (06).
  6. Keep the tab visible. Hidden-tab time is not driven time. Slow PCs are not penalized: event timestamps follow the browser clock.
  7. Public streams & replays. Online rides stream ~25 s behind and stay replayable for 90 days (07). Drivers can mute any chat wallet; spectator road effects are off on ranked runs.
  8. Equal tie-splitting & skill ranking. Rank depends on verified distance before the first mistake, then fewer corrections at a clean finish. Equal results split the combined prize of the tied places equally—never by submission time or wallet address.
  9. Eligibility & compliance. Players must be 18 or older outside excluded jurisdictions, accept that SOL spent on a shift can be lost (not a deposit or an investment), and complete sanctions / KYC verification before USDC prize payouts (09).

HOW A BADGE IS MADE

  1. The server decides. Counts come from the rides the server judged, never from the client. A rejected recording counts only as a ride started. Unlock rows are append-only and badge ids are frozen.
  2. An hour later, a second look. The mint job waits an hour, re-checks the facts (a ride rejected in the meantime no longer counts) and skips demo wallets.
  3. One key that can only mint. A dedicated key, separate from the pool crank and payout key, mints through Metaplex Bubblegum v2 into one Merkle tree (depth 16: 65,536 badges for ~0.7 SOL rent). The player signs nothing and pays nothing.
  4. Crash-safe. Every mint is an outbox row (planned, sent, minted, failed, skipped). The signature is stored before broadcast; after a crash the job waits for blockhash expiry and checks the signature before signing a new one.
  5. Soulbound by the chain. An MPL-Core collection with the permanent freeze plugin and authority removed refuses transfers on chain ("Collection is frozen").
  6. Art and metadata on Arweave (via Irys): generated SVGs/PNGs, descriptive traits, zero royalties. The 34 original badges are live on devnet; the Convoy and free-level ladders unlock off chain and mint once their URIs are uploaded.

Verify it yourself. Collection 56ijQ5…XoMC and tree 45NKrZ…M336 are on devnet. Each minted badge links its asset and mint transaction.

HOW A POOL'S MONEY IS SPLIT (EXAMPLE)

A weekly contest pool combines fixed base funding F = B + S (operator base B stepping $300 → $750 → $1,500 → $3,000 across Season 1 plus sponsorships S), a player-derived performance bonus P = min(0.40 × R_prev, $2,500, 0.90 × F) so player-derived funding is always below 50% (≤ 47.4%) of F + P, and incoming rollover C ≤ 25% × F. Its split is fixed in basis points at creation: 85 % prizes · 5 % seeds the next pool · 10 % fees (Season Final reserve). Shares round down and the remainder goes to fees.

06

Telemetry, validation & anti-cheat

The server rebuilds the road and checks the submitted evidence against shared simulation rules before it records a verdict.

Release snapshot · 17 Oct 2026. Rules v7 are built, awaiting deployment. The public app release checked today uses rules v4. Production defaults to shadow mode: verdicts are recorded and shown, while paid boards ignore them. The program upgrade is separate (05). Full codes and thresholds: docs/tech/anti-cheat/.

NINE LAYERS, FROM CHEAPEST TO STRONGEST

  1. 1SERVER-TIMED SHIFTWall-clock bounds on time and distance; 150 s without a heartbeat ends the ride.
  2. 2A CHECKABLE RECORDINGSHA-256 links bind each batch to its predecessor and ride; accepted batches form an RFC 6962 Merkle root.
  3. 3PHYSICS BOUNDSThe governor, motion, input and sample-order checks reject impossible traces.
  4. 4AGREEMENT WITH THE RIDEThe recording must cover the reported drive and end near its reported distance.
  5. 5SHARED WORLD, REQUIRED TRAFFIC EVIDENCEThe server rebuilds the ride's road and traffic. New rides must supply frame evidence; ignored contacts reject.
  6. 6BOUNDED WORKUntrusted samples are bounded before road lookups; wall time limits batches and canonical bytes.
  7. 7HUMAN TIMINGUniform holds and repeated rhythms only flag; a statistic cannot prove cheating.
  8. 8CHALLENGE CHANNELThe protocol supports timed prompts. Its kinds table is empty today, so none are issued.
  9. 9SIGNED REVIEW & SETTLEMENTAdmins sign decisions. Program v5 commits a public winners root and per-ride pledge attestations; its devnet upgrade is pending.

TWO RECORDINGS OF THE SAME DRIVE

TRUCK · 10 Hz

16 numbers: position, heading, speed, rpm, gear, hull, pedals, clock, switches, controls and fork road. The first twelve feed summary checks; the replay columns carry the heater and cooling state for spectators.

INPUT EVENTS

Key or button, down/up, and the browser event time. At most 2000 per batch. A slow frame does not turn a normal tap into an instant release.

TRAFFIC · EVERY FRAME

Lossless timing, pre-contact truck pose and trailer angle, speed, fork choice, and hull before/after contact. This evidence is inside the batch hash. Display-only car tracks are not evidence.

For required-proof rides, the client seals a batch after 2 game seconds or 500 frames, whichever comes first. Legacy recordings used 15-second batches. The server stamps trafficVersion=1 at start; dropping optional replay columns cannot select legacy rules. TRAFFIC_PROOF_REQUIRED defaults to true.

Never recorded: your screen, other tabs or apps, microphone or camera. A training ride records the drive, the same way a paid ride does.

WHAT THE HASH PROVES

The server recomputes each batch's SHA-256 and checks the chain. Accepted hashes form an RFC 6962 Merkle root, naming the recording used for a verdict or pledge attestation. A modified client can recompute hashes. Hash integrity alone proves neither honest driving nor a human driver; simulation and consistency checks must still pass.

THE SERVER'S ROAD & TRAFFIC

@freightchain/shared supplies the road geometry, route curves, traffic AI and longitudinal physics used by browser and backend. The server supplies the ride's seed, route, trip and assist setting. For new rides it runs traffic at the recorded frame timing, including signals, spawning, hole avoidance, and cab/trailer contact. It checks the hull response and binds every truck sample to a simulated post-contact frame.

The game drives over the server's obstacle plan. Each axle entering a pit costs dmg × (v/28)² – 14 % for the front axle in a sinkhole at 28 m/s, rear axles × 0.6. A measured pass must show at least a quarter of that loss. Two or more holes driven through untouched reject; one flags. Off-road time and stamped spectator donations are checked too.

NEW RIDES · VERSION 1

Required evidence

Missing, malformed, uncovered or inconsistent frames reject. Deleting cars or ignoring a required collision response also rejects, even with rebuilt hashes.

HISTORICAL · VERSION 0

Approximate reconstruction

Old 10 Hz samples lack exact frame timing and trailer angle. Their traffic discrepancies flag for review. Carried coasting estimates also remain diagnostic.

CONTROLLED BROWSER TESTS

The 16 Oct local run used headless Chrome, the real game, real batch uploads, Nest and PostgreSQL. Saved verdicts and replays were checked for claimed finishes; a real collision separately checked the failed outcome. Failed is an actual ride failure; rejected is the validator refusing a claimed finish.

The command is bash .claude/skills/run-tests/e2e.sh traffic. Shared-kernel parity is checked separately against captured browser frames across five routes and three trips. Neither check establishes full-length production load capacity.

CLOCK, WORK & UPLOAD LIMITS

  • Server time bounds the recording. The game clock cannot buy unlimited distance, frames or canonical bytes. Invalid values and excessive travel are refused before geometry work.
  • Protocol-specific budgets. Required traffic evidence gets a larger byte and batch budget than historical recordings; it keeps the same 96 kB canonical batch cap, 400 samples and 2000 events.
  • Races & finish retries: uploads and judging share the player's row lock. Ending waits up to 3 s for the last upload, the game retries for 55 s, and the server accepts batches for 60 s.

CHALLENGE CHANNEL

The heartbeat can issue a prompt about every 240 s of driving, with a 20 s response window. The kinds table is empty today, so none are issued. When enabled, the judge looks for that key within 20 s: Half or more of 3+ prompts ignored rejects; fewer misses flag. A median answer under 80 ms after the server issued the prompt flags.

WHO REVIEWS, AND HOW THAT POWER IS LOCKED

  1. 1OWN SITE + PASSWORDThe admin page lives on its own address behind a password. The admin API answers nowhere else.
  2. 2ADMIN WALLETOnly listed wallets can sign in, for 2 hours. A player login opens nothing.
  3. 3EVERY DECISION SIGNEDAccept, reject and re-judge are each signed by the admin's wallet for that exact ride and decision.
  4. 4AUDIT LOGWho decided what, when, why, and the signature are kept for good. Late uploads never overturn a human decision.
  5. 5WINNERS LOCKED ON CHAINThe payout key commits verified winners as one Merkle root (rank, wallet, amount), public through the 48 h review and subject to cold-admin veto before payout.

LIMITS, SAID PLAINLY

  • Client-reported state & float physics. Shared traffic responses at recorded timing reject tampered traces, while carried coasting estimates flag; an input-only fixed-point replay core and a captcha before a paid shift remain on the roadmap.
  • Off-chain judgement & retention. The Solana program checks settlement rules, signatures and Merkle proofs, not driving physics. Recordings are kept 90 days; the sweep deletes the sign-in log after 180 days.
