# FreightChain — can the Pre-season start without an operator legal entity

Status: research of 17.10.2026, updated (section 7 — Serbia) after the owner's answer; not committed to git. **This is not legal or tax advice.** The answer depends on the owner's country of tax residence and citizenship; the facts were received on 17.10.2026 — see section 7 (Serbia). **[unverified]** — the primary source was not found, or it is ambiguous. **[PROPOSAL]** — my recommendation.

Pre-season context: a devnet game, registration on 2 November, prize week 9–15 November 2026. Entry is free; there are no paid tickets. One attempt per wallet after the airdrop tasks. $500 USDC from the owner's own funds is paid on mainnet to the top 20 (100 / 70 / 50 / 2×30 / 5×20 / 10×12). Players from KZ, UZ, KG, AM, AZ, GE, MD, and UA receive prizes; RU and BY play with no prizes until a consultation with a lawyer. Winners get KYC through Didit and a Chainalysis sanctions check.

## 0. Short answer

**Most likely, yes: the Pre-season can be run in the name of a natural-person organizer.** Conditions:
- Entry really is free (there is no "consideration").
- Winners are determined by skill, not by chance.
- Rules are published that name the organizer and a contact.
- The total fund is capped ($500), and there is KYC and a sanctions check.

**But:**
1. Liability is **personal and unlimited**: claims, taxes, and sanctions violations fall on the owner personally. If the team operates "as a team" with no legal entity, its members may be held to be partners with joint and several liability (Ooki DAO, Sarcuni v. bZx, sec. 1.3).
2. The sanctions regime and the organizer's taxes are set by **the organizer's** citizenship and residence (sec. 3).
3. Before Season 1 (paid tickets), and all the more before the TEN4 TGE, a legal entity (or several) is **required** (sec. 4).

## 1. A natural person and a free skill contest

### 1.1 General principles (the United States as the example; other countries are similar, but not the same)
- An illegal private lottery is the combination of three elements: **prize + chance + consideration**. Remove any one and it is no longer a lottery. **Sweepstakes** = prize + chance, with no payment; a **skill contest** = prize + skill (a fee is sometimes allowed, but state rules differ) ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/launching-sweepstakes-or-contest-what-you-need-to-know-part-1), [Sideman & Bancroft](https://www.sideman.com/contests-and-sweepstakes-101/), [USPIS](https://www.uspis.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/pub-546_consumers-guide-to-sweepstakes-lotteries_508.pdf)).
- We have neither chance (it is skill) nor a fee (it is free). On the general principle this is neither a lottery nor gambling.
- **The caveat on "non-monetary consideration":** in some jurisdictions a participant's substantial effort (follows, reposts, tasks) can count as consideration **[unverified for each country]**. Mitigation: the airdrop tasks are not required **in order to enter the contest** — or say expressly in the rules that entry to the prize week is possible without the social tasks.
- Promo-contest rules are needed in any case: the organizer, the dates, who may enter, how winners are determined, the prizes, geo limits, KYC, taxes, privacy, disputes. For a skill contest the rules are the main protective document.
- The **X/Telegram/Discord platform rules** on promotions apply as well. We do not require likes in exchange for a reward (already decided).

### 1.2 Taxes: the organizer and the winner
- The **winner** almost everywhere declares the prize as income themselves (in the US, even a $50 prize, Schedule 1; see `tax_thresholds.md`). CIS countries differ: in some the duty sits only with the recipient; in others the organizer can be a withholding agent **[depends on the country, unverified]**.
- **A natural-person organizer:** the $500 is a personal expense (a gift or a prize). It usually cannot be deducted as a business expense without individual-entrepreneur status or a legal entity. In some countries the organizer of a drawing has a withholding agent's duties when paying prizes to residents **[depends on the owner's country]**.
- **Forms W-9 / W-8BEN** matter only if the organizer is a **US person** or the payout is treated as US-source income. If the owner is not a US resident, collecting W forms achieves nothing and only adds personal data. This item is conditional; we decide it after the answer to sec. 6.

### 1.3 How others do it
- **Superteam Earn:** on a Superteam or Solana bounty, winners pass KYC and fill in a payment form; payout takes about 7 days. On bounties from outside sponsors (including individuals and companies) the sponsor pays the winner's wallet and may request an invoice or KYC under the sponsor's own rules ([FAQ](https://docs.superteam.fun/the-superteam-handbook/community/faqs/superteam-earn-faq)). The platform allows sponsors of different kinds, but it itself operates through its own legal entity (ToS, [terms](https://superteam.fun/earn/terms-of-use.pdf)).
- **Colosseum:** a company runs the hackathons. Winners sign a prize acceptance, go through due diligence or KYC, and the team lead receives the prize ([rules](https://colosseum.com/legal/Solana%20Frontier%20Hackathon%20Rules.pdf), [ToS](https://colosseum.com/legal/Colosseum%20-%20Terms%20of%20Service.pdf)).
- **Gitcoin:** KYC for grantees with matching > $15,000 ([support](https://support.gitcoin.co/gitcoin-knowledge-base/gitcoin-grants/gitcoins-kyc)). Small amounts — no KYC.
- **Galxe campaigns and DAOs:** the project or its foundation usually issues the rewards. The platform itself is a legal entity; the project is responsible for the campaign terms. I did not find a public practice of "a natural person with no legal entity" among large projects **[unverified]**. Small bounties from private sponsors on Superteam are the closest example.
- **The risk of a "team with no legal entity":**
  - **CFTC v. Ooki DAO (2023):** the court held the DAO to be an **unincorporated association**, that is a "person" under the CEA. The default judgment: $643,542 and prohibitions ([judgment](https://www.cftc.gov/media/8741/enfookidaojudgment060923/download), [order](https://www.cftc.gov/media/8736/enfookidaoorder060923/download)). The founders were personally liable in a related case, and Commissioner Mersinger objected to the theory of liability based on token voting ([statement](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/SpeechesTestimony/mersingerstatement092222)).
  - **Sarcuni v. bZx DAO (S.D. Cal., 2023):** the court found it plausible that token holders with voting rights are a **general partnership** (joint and several liability), and the negligence claim continued ([order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.577791/gov.uscourts.nysd.577791.87.1.pdf), [overview](https://www.fintechanddigitalassets.com/2023/07/whats-next-for-daos-in-the-wake-of-the-ooki-decision/)).
  - **Conclusion:** the formula "FreightChain team" in the rules **does not protect**. Better to name one natural person as the organizer outright than an undefined "team": there is then less chance that every participant is held to be a partner.

## 7. Serbia: a resident of Serbia with Russian citizenship, organizing as a natural person

The starting facts: a tax resident of Serbia, a Serbian residence permit, a Russian citizen, with no status in the US or the EU. The person organizes the devnet Pre-season personally. This is not legal advice, and Serbian law is the main open question here (sec. 7.8).

### 7.1 Games of Chance Act (Zakon o igrama na sreću, ZIS)
The source is the text of the act on the Uprava za igre na sreću website: [PDF](https://uis.gov.rs/static/uploads/13_Zakon_o_igrama_na_srecu_03032020.pdf).
- **Art. 2:** games of chance are games in which participants, **for a fee** ("uz naplatu"), are given a chance of a win, and the result **does not depend on knowledge or skill** but on chance. The Pre-season has **no fee and no chance** (it is skill) — on the literal text, it **does not fall under** this definition. Art. 2 also has a separate exception for public contests of knowledge and skill held before a commission. That exception is narrow (in-person participation before a commission) and does not directly describe an online tournament.
- **Art. 16, "prize games in goods and services" (nagradne igre u robi i uslugama):** games that a **legal entity or an entrepreneur (preduzetnik)** runs **for advertising and other purposes**. Every participant is given an **equal chance** to receive a prize in goods or services that was fixed in advance; one takes part by using the organizer's service or buying the organizer's goods. Required: **consent from the Uprava** (Art. 21), rules, publication of the rules in a daily newspaper 8 days ahead, and a report of the results ([regulation, English](https://uis.gov.rs/static/uploads/3260_Regulation_on_the_manner_of_determining_the_fulfilment_....pdf)). The application is filed no later than 30 days before the start, and the duration is up to 45 days (per a search summary, **[not checked against the original regulation]**). **The levy is 25% of the value of the prize fund** (Art. 107; on $500 that is ~$125), payable no later than 3 days before the start.
- **Conclusion:** a prize game under Art. 16 is an "equal chance" mechanic (in effect, a drawing) run by a legal entity or an entrepreneur, for advertising. A skill tournament ranked by result **probably does not fall under** that description. A natural person is not allowed to run such a game in any event, so the question is classification, not a permit. **Risk:** the Pre-season plainly promotes FreightChain; if the Uprava treats it as a prize game, a natural-person organizer would have no right to run it, and the 25% levy would apply on top. Question no. 1 for a Serbian lawyer. Mitigation: the rules stress skill (the best verified result, deterministic scoring, no drawing of lots, a tie-break that is not random), with no purchase and no "equal chance for everyone".
- In addition: USDC is "digital property". Whether it is goods or a service within the meaning of Art. 16 is **[unverified]**.
- **Consumer protection** (Zakon o zaštiti potrošača): the rules and the advertising must be fair and must not mislead; whether this applies to a natural person who is not a "trgovac" (trader) is **[unverified]**.

### 7.2 Digital Assets Act (Zakon o digitalnoj imovini, SG 153/2020 (Službeni glasnik, Official Gazette), in force from June 2021)
- A license from the NBS (virtual currencies) or from the Securities Commission (tokens) is required of a **"digital asset service provider", which is a legal entity** providing the services listed in the act: exchange, custody, transfer **for others**, a platform, brokerage, and so on ([act, English](https://www.nbs.rs/export/sites/NBS%5Fsite/documents-eng/propisi/zakoni/digitalna%5Fimovina%5Fe.pdf), [NBS](https://www.nbs.rs/en/ciljevi-i-funkcije/nadzor-nad-finansijskim-institucijama/digital-imo/)). Operating without a license is a criminal offence.
- The act allows **OTC transactions with no provider** ([Sovilj 2021](https://rlr.iup.rs/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/18-Sovilj.pdf), [GLI 2026](https://www.globallegalinsights.com/practice-areas/blockchain-cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations/serbia/)). **A one-off payout of one's own USDC** from one's own wallet to 20 winners is not a service to third parties. On the text of the act, this is probably **not a licensable activity** **[check with a lawyer]**.
- USDC is more likely a "virtual currency" (NBS supervision). Issuing stablecoins in Serbia is regulated separately (a white paper), but that does not apply to paying out prizes.
- Buying USDC: Serbia has NBS-licensed providers — VESCON (Crypto12), MCM 965 (ECD), and AX CRYPTO ([NBS register](https://www.nbs.rs/en/ciljevi-i-funkcije/nadzor-nad-finansijskim-institucijama/digital-imo/reg_di/index.html)). ECD sells USDC for RSD at a fee of 0.95% ([ECD](https://ecd.rs/en/faqs/what-are-the-costs-limits-related-to-transactions-through-ecd-wallet/)). **[PROPOSAL]:** buy the $500 of USDC from a licensed Serbian provider and keep the receipt: that produces a document of the acquisition cost (sec. 7.3) and puts the provider in Serbian jurisdiction (EU Art. 5b does not reach it).

### 7.3 Taxes in Serbia
**Winners (not residents of Serbia):**
- The tax on winnings (Art. 83 of the ZPDG, Zakon o porezu na dohodak građana, the Personal Income Tax Law: 20% above the exempt threshold, withheld by the organizer) applies to **games of chance under the ZIS** ([Art. 83](https://odredba.com/zakon/zakon-o-porezu-na-dohodak-gradjana/clan-83/)). If the Pre-season is not a game of chance (sec. 7.1), Art. 83 does not apply.
- Prizes for competitions are usually **"other income" (Art. 85)**: 20% on the base after standardized expenses ([Art. 85](https://odredba.com/zakon/zakon-o-porezu-na-dohodak-gradjana/clan-85/)). If a **natural person** pays (rather than a legal entity or an entrepreneur), there is usually no withholding at source — the **resident** recipient files their own return (PP OPO) ([Biznis.rs](https://biznis.rs/vesti/srbija/kako-baksis-preko-aplikacije-postaje-poreska-obaveza/), a summary). For **nonresidents**, Serbian tax depends on whether the income is treated as derived in Serbia; for sports prizes that is set by where the competition is held ([Gecić Law](https://www.geciclaw.com/sr/poreske-obaveze-sportista/)). For an online tournament with the organizer in Serbia — **[unverified; a question for a Serbian tax adviser]**. ### 7.4 Russian citizenship
