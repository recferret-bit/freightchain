03 · Project fit, utility and demand

The strongest fit is earned identity. A verified paid drive earns a capped token, cosmetics express that achievement, and a 1-HONK chat line is legible to spectators. SOL tickets and prizes remain independent of token volatility. Free rides emit zero by tier; this is not the generic free-wallet faucet assumed by the skill.

The weakest fit is paid influence over a competitive drive. Time and weather feed the climate simulation. A second wallet bypasses the own-driver ban, so collusion remains possible. Retain harmless social and cosmetic utility while resolving this physics coupling. The current branch checks recording summaries, input timing and road facts; the skill’s 10 Hz ReplayEngine language describes a stronger architecture than this branch contains.

The demand loop needs purchased tokens to work. The chain/ledger split currently prevents a new spectator from using market-acquired tokens normally. After correcting settlement, distinguish one-off collectors from recurring spectators. A full garage costs 4,485 HONK; 100% burns reduce current supply but never restore emission capacity. There is no preminted community reward vault to recycle into, so the skill’s 50/35/15 router and reward-vault starvation diagnosis are not adopted.

Supply competition: nominal hourly rewards are 100 / 125 / 250 HONK for 1h / 4h / 8h finishes. Face-price ticket spend per HONK is $0.05 / $0.03 / $0.025; these are not market prices or arbitrage floors. Prize recovery, success probability, time, automation and fees change actual farming cost. Verified paid eligibility helps but is not a Sybil proof.

Narrative and marketing proposal: “Finish the haul. Earn HONK. Wear the cab you earned.” Show an actual verified receipt → confirmed token → cosmetic transformation, with network and pending status visible. Use short driver/spectator clips and small creator sessions to measure free-start → retained driver → verified paid finish → first sink → 30-day repeat sink. Reward creators for retained participants and verified usage, not trading volume; avoid liquid upfront token grants. No invented token allocation, guaranteed return or unverified ‘bot-proof’ claim.

Pass 2 of 3 · Attack the code and the first-pass proposals

Tested the commit-to-chain failure boundary, reused a confirmed burn receipt and compared actual climate updates. A retry without a persistent outbox is insufficient; a preflight eligibility check cannot make a later burn refundable; per-wallet limits cannot prevent coordinated climate manipulation. Adding a TWAP to the small native pool would introduce price-manipulation risk, and a bond-based paid leaderboard multiplier would violate fairness. The proposed hardening therefore uses durable orders, unique receipts, secured issuance and presentation-only competitive effects.

A 10× token price raises the fiat/SOL cost of fixed-token sinks 10×; an 80% crash reduces it to one fifth. The 20-second effect window limits frequency, not competitive advantage or affordability. Chat has a 10-second per-wallet/per-ride gap. Maximum possible button spam is not a demand forecast.

Pass 3 of 3 · 36-month equilibrium and constrained blueprint

Replaced the generator’s invented 1B supply, team cliff and automatic bond curve with HONK’s 100M cap, 2M genesis, activity-based 98M issuance, permanent burns and zero implemented bonds. The illustrative base case absorbs 15.4% of issuance; it does not reach S/F≥1 at M12. There is no Month-13 insider unlock. The high-activity case exhausts emission capacity during month 27, after which paid rides continue without new HONK unless the product policy changes. Proposed explicit post-cap UX and measured demand gates, not silent retuning.

06 · Complete issue register and concrete proposals

CRITICAL · H01

Any signer can take the entire LP position after day 365

ReleaseLp accepts an arbitrary recipient signer and transfers the entire locker balance to that signer’s ATA. Pool stores no beneficiary. After unlock, an unrelated wallet can call release_lp then withdraw_lp for the whole LP supply, taking both reserves. The time lock delays the theft; it does not authorize the recipient.

Proposal. Store and enforce the intended LP beneficiary at initialization, or irrevocably burn the LP if permanent liquidity is the intended policy. Define the legitimate owner’s withdrawal rights explicitly.

Acceptance evidence. A distinct attacker signer must fail at unlock and after it; the authorized recipient can redeem only its own share.

HIGH · H02

First initializer selects the canonical emission authority

initialize uses fixed global PDAs, accepts any payer with 10 SOL, and sets config.emitter to that payer. If deployment and intended initialization are separated, a competing initializer can claim the canonical configuration. That key can mint the remaining 98 million HONK within the cap. This is a pre-initialization launch race, not a way for outsiders to replace an already initialized emitter.

Proposal. Authorize the intended initializer in the program or a deployment-bound configuration, and verify configuration ownership before opening access.

Acceptance evidence. An unauthorized initializer fails without moving funds; the intended initializer succeeds once.

HIGH · H03

Committed ledger reward can permanently miss its chain mint

VerdictService commits the ledger credit before calling the chain emitter. If the process stops or RPC fails, a retry finds the existing mint row and returns null; mintRide then skips emission. No durable pending-delivery state is recorded. The adapter also treats an error string containing “already in use” or “0x0” as success without reading the receipt. An outage can leave game credit without spendable SPL tokens.

Proposal. Write a durable emission outbox atomically with the ledger reward. Retry independently; confirm receipt owner, rider and amount. Replace error-string success detection with on-chain reconciliation and track pending/confirmed/failed delivery.

Acceptance evidence. Crash after DB commit, RPC timeout before/after submission, duplicate receipt, and restart all converge to exactly one matching chain mint.

Evidence level: Reproduced against actual TypeScript service code with mocked DB/RPC boundaries.

HIGH · H04

One chain burn receipt buys multiple effects

requireHonkBurn checks signer and amount but never consumes a signature or binds it to a ride/action. HonkBurn has no signature or instruction-index uniqueness. A viewer can reuse the same 10-HONK receipt for later day/night effects, or another ride, while spending only ledger balances on the later requests. The probe accepted one receipt twice: 20 HONK removed from the ledger, only 10 represented on chain. This is bounded by available ledger credit, not an unlimited free faucet.

Proposal. Persist a unique receipt key (network, program, signature, instruction index) and bind it to an order/action. Consume it atomically with the entitlement; retries of the same order return the original result.

Acceptance evidence. Replay a receipt across rides, phases and concurrent requests; exactly one logical order can consume it.

Evidence level: Reproduced against actual time-effect handler with mocked receipt/event stores.

HIGH · H05

A refused effect cannot refund an already executed SPL burn

The client signs and sends the burn before the API validates or inserts the effect. Rate limits, stale rides, insufficient ledger credit, a delayed confirmation, or a busy event window can reject the API call after the wallet burn. reverseBurn only restores PostgreSQL credit. A crash after debit but before event insertion also leaves a debit without delivery because they are separate operations. The spec’s “burn does not land” claim is only true for a successful ledger compensation, not chain supply.

Proposal. Use durable action orders and retryable receipt confirmation/delivery. Validate eligibility before requesting a signature, but do not treat preflight as a concurrency guarantee. For refundable actions use an escrow/transfer-and-settle design; never label restoration of ledger credit as reversal of an SPL burn.

Acceptance evidence. Competing effects, ride termination during signing, RPC indexing lag and process termination preserve a recoverable order and a truthful result.

HIGH · H06

Market tokens and the game balance are separate spendability systems

Swaps and transfers alter SPL balances; GET /api/honk and all game debits use HonkAccount. There is no observed transfer/swap reconciliation into that account. A new spectator can buy HONK, burn it on chain, then receive honk_balance from the game. Conversely, a rider who transfers tokens away retains ledger credit. The intended spectator demand loop therefore does not work as an ordinary fungible-token economy.

Proposal. Choose and document the authoritative chain-mode balance. Grant utility from a unique confirmed on-chain action receipt without requiring a second unrelated earned balance, or implement a fully specified deposit/withdrawal ledger. Keep ledger-only mode explicitly separate.

Acceptance evidence. A new wallet buys tokens, obtains an effect and a part; transfers between two wallets preserve both spendability and total accounting.

HIGH · H07

Checkpoint and admin-acceptance promises do not match the reward trigger

judgeLocked returns null unless the ride is Completed, so a failed ride that passed 20% or 50% never reaches the checkpoint mint. The current test explicitly expects failed rides to remain unjudged. override can change a flagged/rejected ride to verified but never calls HonkMintService; later automatic judging preserves that override and returns minted:null. Domain quote tests do not cover these missing lifecycle paths.

Proposal. Resolve the intended policy for failed checkpoint rides and encode it in verification and issuance. Route every newly eligible verified decision, including admin acceptance, through one idempotent reward transition.

Acceptance evidence. Verified partial crash, first accepted admin override, repeated override, rejection and full finish each have explicit end-to-end expected rewards.

MEDIUM · H08

The ride DTO can claim tokens were minted when none were credited

RideDto calls honkQuote with verdict status alone. honkQuote reports pays=quote for every verified qualifying ride. It does not read the stored mint amount. When the cap is exhausted, credit returns null but the DTO still says minted / pays 100 for a 1h finish; a final clipped reward can likewise be overstated.

Proposal. Populate paid amounts and delivery state from the committed mint/outbox record. Keep hypothetical quote separate from credited and chain-confirmed amounts.

Acceptance evidence. A 1-HONK cap remainder and a fully exhausted cap show exactly 1 and 0 paid, respectively.

HIGH · H09

The cap is enforced; verified-ride provenance remains trusted

mint_ride accepts an emitter-supplied ride id, recipient and arbitrary positive amount, checking only signer, duplicate id and the global cap. It does not verify a paid ride, checkpoint, verdict, or delay. Config has no emitter rotation. RideMint records amount and bump, not recipient/verdict evidence. An emitter compromise can allocate all remaining issuance. Shortened demo rides also receive tier-based rewards because the mint input omits demo/payment provenance. No 10 Hz ReplayEngine exists on this branch; its verdict checks summaries and world facts. Upgrade authority was not inspected on a deployed HONK instance.

Proposal. State the trust boundary accurately. Bind issuance receipts to rider and finalized eligible verdict, exclude demo/test entitlement, introduce durable review-before-delivery, and specify secured emitter rotation and program-upgrade governance. The cap alone is not proof of human driving.

Acceptance evidence. Unauthorized emitter, fabricated ride, demo completion, changed recipient, duplicate delivery and delayed rejection receive explicit tests; inspect upgrade authority before any deployment claim.

CRITICAL · H10

Paid weather and time effects reach competitive physics

assignWeather(snow) changes snow and ambient temperature to at most −8°C. updateClimate uses both for engine cooling, which feeds coldK, reduced power and cold-engine hull damage. Night also changes ambientC. These paid-ride HONK effects bypass LIVE_EFFECTS. A second wallet can assist a driver or impair competitors. This violates the skill’s no-paid-physics invariant even though cosmetics and the 1-HONK chat line remain harmless. A 10-second controlled probe produced hull 98.7786 in clear weather versus 98.4420 after snow, with identical controls and initial truck state.

Proposal. Isolate purchased presentation effects from authoritative thermal/physics inputs on competitive rides, or restrict gameplay-changing effects to a separately disclosed noncompetitive mode. Merely adding a per-wallet cooldown does not address colluding wallets.

Acceptance evidence. Compare full physics traces with and without paid presentation effects; competitive temperature, power, hull and finish outcome must remain identical.

The configured program id is not the emission destination

Why the raw deterministic report is not the final verdict

The parser extracted the token name as “desc” from a table header and left the standard unspecified. Its output scored 74 → 84 → 95, but later passes automatically assume hypothetical fixes, substitute a 1B-token blueprint, add insider allocations/cliffs and a 50/35/15 router, and claim issues are resolved. Those are template assumptions, not observations of HONK. This reviewed report supersedes those conclusions, retains the existing locked numbers, and gives no score credit for unapplied fixes.

Six or nine decimals and suggested launch-float bands are preferences, not general SPL validity laws. PermanentDelegate and transfer-fee concerns are inapplicable to this legacy mint. Zero insider allocation needs no fabricated vesting. A capped ongoing mint can retain a program authority; immediate revocation would prevent the specified 98M ride emission. Content addressing needs actual durable hosting, and a local JSON file is not metadata immutability.

Scope: current working-tree source, including pre-existing uncommitted changes; no economic retuning, application changes, deployment, transactions, or marketing publication.
