Freight Chain · how a shift is checked

Verified Play

Every online shift is recorded while you drive. When it completes, the server checks the recording before the shift can rank on a paid leaderboard. It doesn't replay your drive. It checks that the recording is complete, physically possible, matches the ride, and was driven by a person.

The path of one ride

From your keyboard to the leaderboard

1In your browser

Record

Every key press and release, and the truck's state 10 times a second, while an online ride is on the road.

2Every 15 s

Seal

The game packs the last 15 seconds into a batch, fingerprints it with SHA-256 and links it to the batch before.

3On the server

Store

The server recomputes the fingerprint, checks the batch's shape and limits, measures what the judge will need, and stores it. Stored batches never change.

4When the shift completes

Judge

All batches are checked together: the chain, the physics, the distance, the coverage, the timing of your hands, and whether you answered the dispatch prompts.

5Result

Rank

verified shifts rank. Flagged ones wait for a person. Rejected ones don't rank.

Tamper evidence

The batches form a chain

Each batch carries the fingerprint (SHA-256) of the batch before it. The first one links to the ride's own id. Change one number anywhere and its fingerprint changes, so the next link no longer matches. The server recomputes every fingerprint itself when a batch arrives, so a batch can't claim a fingerprint it doesn't have.

The verdict

Two sets of checks, with different powers

Checks on the recording itself can reject a ride, because a broken or impossible recording proves something is wrong. Checks on how human your timing looks can only flag a ride for a person to review, because statistics can be wrong about a real driver.

The recording

can reject
  • Complete and in orderEvery batch present, every link matching, every fingerprint correct, samples in time order.no-recording · missing-batches · chain-broken · hash-mismatch · sample-out-of-order · bad-sample
  • Under the governorThe truck is limited to 28 m/s (100.8 km/h). Any sample above 29.4 m/s (105.8 km/h) is impossible.over-governor
  • No teleportsBetween two samples the truck can't move further than top speed allows (×1.2, plus 2 m).teleport
  • Ends where the ride endedThe last recorded distance matches the distance the ride reported, within 30 m + 1 %, allowing for seconds whose final upload never arrived.recording-mismatch
  • Covers the driveUnder 50 % of the driven time recorded rejects. Under 90 % only flags.recording-incomplete · recording-gaps (flag)
  • Someone droveA truck that moved more than 50 m needs key presses, and gas among them.no-inputs · no-throttle-input
  • Dispatch prompts answeredAbout every 4 minutes of driving a prompt appears on the windscreen – FLASH YOUR HEADLIGHTS · PRESS L – with a 20 s countdown. The recording must show that key. Ignore half or more of 3+ prompts and the shift rejects; one missed only flags.challenges-ignored · challenge-missed (flag)

Your hands

can only flag
  • Holds varyPeople hold a key for different lengths each time. If one key's holds barely vary at all (spread under 8 %, over 6+ holds), that looks scripted.uniform-holds
  • Rhythm isn't a loopIf the 3 most common gaps between presses (to the millisecond) make up more than 60 % of all gaps, a script is cycling fixed delays.repeated-timings
  • Presses take timeIf more than 30 % of presses are released within 10 ms, they were sent, not pressed.instant-presses
  • Prompts were readIf the prompts were answered under 80 ms after the server sent them (the median), something read the heartbeat and pressed the key – a person has to see the banner first.challenge-reflex
  • Fingerprint missingIf your browser's crypto failed and a batch left without its fingerprint, the server fingerprinted it on arrival – it is kept, and only flagged.unhashed-batch

Fair to real people: with fewer than 8 presses there is no opinion at all. Browsers that round their clock (Firefox privacy mode, Tor) are detected, and the two checks a coarse clock would break are skipped.

Verified

Nothing found. The shift ranks on the paid leaderboard.

Flagged

Something looked off but proves nothing. The shift goes to the review queue and doesn't rank until an admin accepts it.

Rejected

The recording is missing, broken or impossible. The shift doesn't rank. An admin can still look at it and reverse the verdict.

The last minute

A bad connection at the finish doesn't cost you the shift

Ending a shift waits at most 3 seconds for the final upload. If it hasn't gone through, the game keeps retrying every 5 seconds for 55 seconds, and the server keeps accepting batches for 60 seconds after the end. Every late batch makes the server judge the ride again.

Why it is hard to cheat

What someone might try, and what stops it

AttemptWhat happens
Edit the recording after drivingfaster laps, fewer mistakesEvery batch is fingerprinted and chained, and the server recomputes the fingerprint on arrival. An edited batch breaks the chain. A batch already stored can't be replaced: sending the same batch number again is ignored.
Speed hack or teleporta modified gameAny sample above the governor, any jump further than top speed allows, or a final distance that doesn't match the ride rejects the shift.
Cut out the boring hoursA missing batch or a gap in the chain rejects. Recording under half of the driven time rejects. Under 90 % flags.
Upload a made-up recording faster than real timeThe server compares every upload with its own clock since the ride started. A ride can't hold more game time than wall time × 1.05 + 30 s, or more batches than one per 5 s + 10. The minimum shift time is also measured by the server, not by the game.
Drive with a macro or a botMachine-perfect timing shows up in hold lengths, repeated rhythms and instant presses. The shift is flagged and can't rank until a person has looked at it.
Let a bot drive the real gamenobody at the keyboardThe server asks for a key at times it chooses, through the heartbeat. A bot that doesn't read the windscreen never answers; ignoring most prompts rejects the shift.
Script the answersread the heartbeat, press the keyThe answer arrives a few milliseconds after the prompt was issued – a person needs a quarter of a second to read it. Such shifts are flagged for review.
Send a fake recording without a fingerprintThe server fingerprints every batch itself; a missing client fingerprint is only a flag. A wrong one still rejects. Each ride may also hold only as many bytes as the time since its start allows.
Sneak a batch in while the shift endsUploads and judging take the same per-player lock, so every stored batch is part of a verdict. A batch that arrives after the end triggers a new judgement.
Flood the serverBatches are capped at 96 kB, 400 samples and 2000 key events, plus the per-ride limit above and the API rate limits.
A rogue or hijacked admin accountSee below: four locks, and every decision is signed by the admin's wallet and logged.
Who reviews flagged rides

Admin decisions are locked down and on record

Flagged and rejected shifts can be accepted, rejected or re-judged by hand on the admin page. That power is protected in four layers.

1Separate site + passwordThe admin page lives on its own address behind a password. The admin API answers nowhere else.
2Admin wallet sign-inOnly listed wallets can sign in, and only for 2 hours. A normal player login opens nothing.
3Every decision signedEach accept, reject or re-judge is signed by the admin's wallet for that exact ride and decision.
4Audit logWho decided what, when, why (the note), and the signature are stored for good.
For players

What you can count on

  • Only the drive is recordedYour key presses and the truck's state during an online ride. No mouse, screen, camera or other tabs.
  • Statistics never reject youTiming checks only flag. A flagged shift is looked at by a person before anything is decided.
  • A dropped connection at the end is not fatalThe final upload gets about a minute to arrive. If it never does, only coverage is affected.
  • Slow PCs and privacy browsers are not punishedTimes come from the browser's own events, rounded clocks are detected, and short rides get no timing opinion at all.
  • Every verdict has reasonsEach verdict is stored with the checks that fired, what they measured, and the rules version used, so it can always be explained and re-checked.
  • Human decisions stick, and are accountableA late upload never overturns an admin's decision. Re-judging is a deliberate, signed and logged action.
  • Nothing changes under you without noticeRules are versioned. While they are tuned, shadow mode shows verdicts without affecting the leaderboards.
  • Recordings don't live foreverThey are deleted 90 days after the ride. Flagged shifts are kept until someone has reviewed them.
Limits

What this does not do

  • The server doesn't replay your drive.The game runs on your computer and its physics can't be re-run on the server. A careful bot playing the real game with human-like timing could pass the automatic checks. That is why flagged shifts go to a person, and why the checks keep being tightened.
  • The thresholds are first estimates.Rules v2 (the prompt rules included) have not been tuned on real rides yet. Until they are, the leaderboards ignore verdicts (shadow mode). After a change, old rides are judged again at the new rules from the command line.
  • Verdicts decide ranking only.An entry fee goes into the weekly pool whatever the outcome of the ride. Prize payouts are not built yet.
  • A refused batch ends the recording.If the server refuses a malformed batch, the game stops recording for that ride and the reason is logged, so an admin can see why the verdict came out as it did.
