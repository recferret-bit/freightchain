# Freight Chain weekly prize contest — official rules

**DRAFT. Needs legal review.** This is not legal advice, not an offer, and not a final set of rules. Do not publish it as the rules of a live contest. The operator has not opened this contest. No prize shown here is currently being paid.

## 1. What the contest is

The contest is a skill contest. The score is how far a driver gets along a fixed weekly route before the first mistake. It is not a raffle and not a sweepstakes.

Free rides and paid rides are not equal entries. A driver may count two free ranked starts and up to five paid ranked starts each week. Do not describe the free rides as an equal-chance way to win.

The prize is USDC. The TEN4 token is not a prize, not an entry fee, and not a way to raise a score.

The on-chain ticket pool is a separate product. Buying a ticket does not buy a share of this contest's prize.

## 2. Who may enter

A driver must be 18 or older and must say so before a ranked start.

The following may not enter a ranked start and may not receive a prize. The lists are a server setting. The draft defaults are:

- Sanctioned countries, draft: Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria. A lawyer has to replace this list. It is not an OFAC opinion.
- The United Kingdom and the European Union, by default.
- These US states, by default: South Carolina, Arizona, Louisiana, Arkansas, Delaware, Montana, Tennessee.

A driver whose country is unknown is refused. Ranked entry does not wait for identity checks. A prize is not sent until the winner has passed the operator's identity check and a sanctions check. Those checks are not built.

## 3. The week and the boards

A week runs from Monday 19:00 UTC to the next Monday 19:00 UTC.

There are three boards. Sprint is the 1 hour route and receives 25% of that week's contest pool. Haul is the 4 hour route and receives 30%. Long Haul is the 8 hour route and receives 45%.

Every ranked start on a board in a week uses that week's route. Spectator weather, day and night changes, and donated obstacles do not change a ranked run. They may still appear on a run that was not ranked.

## 4. How a start counts

Before the truck moves, the driver chooses ranked or unranked.

Each week, the first two free starts that were declared ranked, and the first five paid starts that were declared ranked, are the ones that can score. The board keeps the best one of those. Later starts, and any start that was not declared in time, do not score. They can still earn TEN4, take donations, and unlock cosmetics. Tickets are not limited to five.

Declaring a start ranked after it has already started does not count and does not use a slot.

## 5. The prize pool

The pool is an operator prize. It is a base plus a bonus.

The base starts at 300 USDC a week. It can step to 750, then 1,500, then 3,000, and only after four weeks in a row in which ticket revenue was more than four times the base then in force. It steps one level at a time.

The bonus is 40% of the previous week's ticket revenue, and not more than 2,500 USDC. The amount is fixed before the week starts. It does not grow with tickets sold during the week. Ten percent of that bonus is held for the season final. The rest of the bonus, and the whole base, is split across the three boards.

TEN4 is not added to the pool.

## 6. How a board is paid

If a board has at least ten drivers, it pays its base and its bonus.

Eighty percent of a board is paid to places. On a board of 100 drivers or fewer the shares are:

| Place | Share of the board |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 25% |
| 2 | 15% |
| 3 | 10% |
| 4 | 7.5% |
| 5 | 7.5% |
| 6 through 10 | 3% each |

Twenty percent is split equally among drivers who finish the route with no mistake. A driver in the top 10 can also take a finisher share. No finisher share is larger than the 10th-place prize. The rest of that 20% is not paid that week.

If more than 100 drivers score, the places extend to the top 10% of the field. The 80% is then split on a straight declining slope. That slope is a draft. A lawyer and the owner can replace it before the contest opens.

A prize under 5 USDC is not sent.

A board with fewer than ten drivers pays from its base only, and only to the places that exist. Its bonus is held for one week. Money that was already held from the week before goes to the season final instead of being held again. There is no growing jackpot.

## 7. Season

A season is eight weeks. On each board, the eight drivers with the best record of weekly places go to a final. Missing a week counts against that record. The final is one route, with spectator road effects off. The final is not built. Ten percent of each week's bonus is what funds it.

## 8. What is not live

No week is open. No USDC prize is being paid. Identity checks, sanctions screening, and the bank transfer are not connected. The route, the ranked button, and the switch that ignores spectator events on a ranked run are specified here and not yet enforced in the game. Until they are, this document is a draft for review, not the rules of a contest a driver can enter.
